## Problem Statement
Software engineers struggle to effectively communicate their technical capabilities, problem-solving skills, and engineering rigor through traditional resumes/CVs. Recruiters and hiring managers face difficulty verifying claims and assessing true engineering depth, leading to mismatched expectations, increased hiring risk, and inefficient screening processes. Engineers lack a standardized way to present verifiable evidence of their work, architectural decisions, and learning over time.

## Stakeholder Pain Points
- Recruiters: Cannot quickly verify skill claims, spend excessive time on screening, risk presenting unqualified candidates to hiring managers.
- Hiring Managers: Difficulty assessing technical fit and engineering judgment, rely on interviews that may not reveal true capability, risk of costly hiring mistakes.
- Technical Interviewers: Spend time verifying basics instead of evaluating deeper engineering skills, inconsistent evaluation criteria.
- Engineers: Unable to showcase engineering process and decision-making, rely on superficial project lists, difficulty demonstrating growth over time.
- Clients: Difficulty assessing relevance of past work and ability to solve specific problems, rely on trust rather than evidence.

## Business Impact
- Increased time-to-hire and associated costs.
- Higher risk of poor technical hires affecting team productivity and project outcomes.
- Engineers miss opportunities due to inability to effectively demonstrate capabilities.
- Reduced trust in engineering credentials in the hiring process.
